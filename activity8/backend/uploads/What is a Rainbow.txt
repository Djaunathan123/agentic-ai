What is a Rainbow?

A rainbow is a natural phenomenon that appears in the sky when sunlight passes through tiny water droplets in the air. The droplets refract, reflect, and disperse the sunlight into different colors, creating a colorful arc.

A rainbow has seven commonly recognized colors:
1. Red
2. Orange
3. Yellow
4. Green
5. Blue
6. Indigo
7. Violet

Rainbows usually appear after rain when the sun shines while water droplets are still present in the atmosphere. The sun must be behind the observer, and the rain should be in front of them.

There are different types of rainbows, including primary rainbows, secondary rainbows, and double rainbows. A secondary rainbow appears outside the primary rainbow and has its colors in reverse order.

Interesting Facts:
- Every rainbow is unique because it depends on the position of the observer and the water droplets.
- A rainbow is actually a full circle, but from the ground we usually only see an arc.
- Rainbows can also be seen near waterfalls, fountains, or mist where sunlight interacts with tiny water droplets.
